About

I am a postdoctoral fellow at Missouri University of Science and Technology. I work where thermal plasma meets high-temperature metallurgy.

Right now I am using plasma processing to recover critical minerals from spent lithium-ion batteries and rare earth magnets.

My training is in plasma engineering. I spent my PhD at the Institute for Plasma Research building and diagnosing DC plasma torches for low-pressure spraying. Since then I have pointed the same tools at a different problem: getting metal out of its oxide with hydrogen.

Before Missouri I was a postdoctoral researcher at Arizona State University, in the group of Prof. Seetharaman Sridhar.

Research

Critical minerals from waste

My current work at Missouri S&T. I use plasma processing to recover critical metals from spent lithium-ion batteries and from rare earth magnets. The same arc that strips oxygen from iron ore can pull valuable metals out of the waste stream.

Spent Li-ion battery black mass, rare earth magnets.

Hydrogen plasma smelting reduction

One step from ore to liquid iron. A hydrogen arc does the heating and the chemistry at once. I study the plasma and melt interface, because that thin layer sets the reduction rate.

Transferred arcs, argon and hydrogen atmospheres, liquid oxide melts.

Plasma diagnostics

Optical emission spectroscopy, infrared thermography and plain electrical measurements. Used together, they map temperature and species through the arc and track metallization while it happens.

Emission spectroscopy, infrared thermography, arc voltage signatures.

Hydrogen reduction across the melting range

Iron ore reduction with hydrogen from 900 to 1590 °C. Solid, mixed and fully liquid. The rate does not climb smoothly with temperature, and the reason matters for process design.

Solid state through to liquid slag.

Plasma torch and reactor engineering

DC arc and RF plasma reactors, designed and characterized from the electrode up. My PhD covered low-pressure DC plasma spray torches from 1 to 30 mbar, with similarity theory to predict how a torch behaves. A product patent on a DC plasma spray torch is under review.

DC torches, RF plasma, thermal spray coatings, vacuum systems.

Beyond iron

Reduction of aluminum, magnesium and titanium oxides. Hydrogen generation by methane pyrolysis.

Notes on plasma

Short takes. Each one is tied to a paper.

The arc is not a uniform heater.

Most process models treat the arc as one hot, even source. It is not. Our measurements show layers: a very hot argon core, a cooler hydrogen-rich mantle and a comparatively cool boundary layer sitting on the melt. The chemistry is decided in that last layer.

From arXiv:2607.06840

Ions get the attention. Neutrals do the work.

Positive hydrogen ions recombine before they reach the melt surface. What arrives at the oxide is mostly neutral atomic hydrogen and vibrationally excited H2, not energetic ions.

From arXiv:2607.06840

Your power supply is already a sensor.

Arc voltage drops sharply as metallization nears 95%. Metallic iron makes the sample conductive, and iron vapor makes the arc itself more conductive. You can watch reduction finish on a voltmeter.

From Advanced Sustainable Systems (2026)

Hotter is not always faster.

In 20 minutes of hydrogen, reduction climbs from about 35% at 900 °C to over 90% at 1550 °C. Between 1100 and 1400 °C it stalls near 60%. Temperature alone does not buy you rate.

From ACS Sustainable Chemistry & Engineering (2025)
